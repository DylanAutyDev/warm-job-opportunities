# Research Methodology

This file provides additional detail for agents conducting job searches. Follow the main instructions in `SKILL.md`.

## Source hierarchy

Use sources in roughly this order:

1. Employer's own careers page.
2. Employer's official ATS application page.
3. Employer's own blog, newsroom or engineering site.
4. A public post from the founder, CTO, engineering manager or named hiring manager.
5. Reputable technical communities and startup networks.
6. General job boards as discovery sources only.

A job board can help discover a vacancy. It does not replace employer verification.

## Vacancy verification checklist

For every recommended vacancy, record:

- Employer name and official website.
- Exact title and team or product area.
- Direct employer or official ATS URL.
- Whether the application page loads and accepts applications.
- Date checked.
- Whether the employer still presents the role as open.
- Whether the role is permanent and full-time.
- Remote arrangement.
- Country eligibility evidence.
- Time-zone or working-hour requirements.
- Salary and benefits if published.
- Relevant requirements and candidate gaps.
- Any stale-listing or active-hiring uncertainty.

Do not infer country eligibility from:
- The word "remote".
- An employee's location.
- A company's global customer base.
- A job board's location tag.
- A third-party recruiter claim.

## Active hiring versus live listing

Use precise language:

- "Application page is live": the page exists and the application route appears usable.
- "Employer lists the role as open": the official careers page currently advertises it.
- "Actively interviewing": only use when the employer or a credible current source confirms it.
- "Hiring status unclear": use when the page is live but freshness or active hiring cannot be established.

A recently reposted role is not automatically a new vacancy. Check whether it is a refreshed listing, a duplicate or a genuinely reopened position.

## Warm lead evidence

Strong signals:
- A founder says they are hiring a backend engineer and names the role or responsibilities.
- An engineering leader shares a current job description and invites direct applications.
- A company announcement names a specific engineering vacancy.
- A hiring manager confirms that a particular team is adding an engineer.

Weak signals:
- A company has recently raised funding but has no stated vacancy.
- An executive says the company is growing.
- A person has a technical title and accepts connection requests.
- A recruiter says they have opportunities without naming the employer.
- An old job post with no evidence it remains open.

Do not present weak signals as warm leads. If useful, label them as companies to monitor, not current opportunities.

## Candidate fit assessment

Evaluate the work rather than matching keywords mechanically.

Consider:
- How much of the core stack the candidate already knows.
- Whether the candidate has delivered similar systems.
- Whether adjacent skills transfer naturally.
- Whether the role is mostly implementation, architecture, operations or management.
- Whether the seniority expectations are realistic.
- Whether a new framework or language is a reasonable ramp-up.
- Whether the role's domain is learnable.
- Whether the candidate's experience is likely to pass initial screening.

Be transparent about material gaps. Do not describe a role as an excellent fit when a mandatory requirement is missing.

## Stability and growth

Career stability is not guaranteed by company size or funding alone. Look for evidence such as:
- A real product with customers or revenue.
- A sustainable business model.
- Clear ownership and engineering team structure.
- Reasonable hiring and interview practices.
- A role with ongoing product work rather than a short project disguised as employment.
- Opportunities to deepen technical skills or take on broader ownership.
- Transparent employment terms and compensation.

State uncertainty where financial or organizational information is unavailable.

## Outreach quality

When suggesting outreach:
- Contact the person most directly connected to the vacancy.
- Use a public and credible contact route.
- Explain why the candidate is relevant to this specific opening.
- Keep the first message short and human.
- Do not claim a referral, introduction or prior relationship that does not exist.
- Do not guess email formats.
- Avoid mass outreach templates that could be sent to any company.

## Search coverage

Search beyond mainstream aggregators. Depending on available tools, explore:
- Company career pages and official ATS boards.
- Founder and engineering-leader posts.
- Startup directories and accelerator portfolios.
- Product and engineering communities.
- Open-source communities where companies announce hiring.
- Company blogs and release announcements.
- Remote-first company directories.

Do not sacrifice verification quality to increase the number of sources or results.
